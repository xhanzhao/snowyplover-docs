Camera moves

There are two ways to move the camera. Keyframes fly through views you saved by hand. Procedural moves take one camera and move it along a path: around the target, toward it, up into plan.

Where a camera comes from

  • [[Get Camera From Named Views]] reads Rhino Named Views by name. It gives one camera per name, in the order you list them.
  • [[Get Camera From Viewport]] reads the active viewport. Click Update after you move the view.
  • [[Construct Camera]] builds one from a location, a target, a lens length and an up vector.

Every camera ends up in [[Apply Camera]], which writes it to the active viewport while its button reads Active. Right-click it to force a projection: perspective, two-point perspective or parallel.

Keyframes

[[Animate Keyframes]] interpolates a list of cameras with one t. This is the move from Your first animation.

nv  = GetCameraFromNamedviews [namedViews]
t   = slider 0.5
key = AnimateKeyframes [cameras, t, durations, mode, curvature]
cam = ApplyCamera [camera]

nv.camera -> key.cameras
t ~> key.t
key.camera -> cam.camera
  • durations: a relative weight per segment. Leave it empty for equal segments.
  • mode: Linear (0) goes straight from one camera to the next; Smooth (1) curves through all camera positions. Right-click to set it.
  • curvature: with Smooth, 0 gives straight segments, 1 a natural curve, up to 3 for broad arcs.

Procedural moves

Each of these takes a camera and a t and returns a moved camera. At t = 0 they return the camera unchanged, so the move starts exactly where the input camera is.

ComponentWhat it doesExtra inputs
[[Orbit Camera]]One full circle around the target.radius, heightScaler
[[Dolly Track Zoom In]]Moves the camera toward the target, or toward targetPoint.stopDistance
[[Dolly Track Zoom Out]]Moves the camera away from the target.stopDistance
[[Dolly Zoom]]Moves the camera back while narrowing the lens, so the subject keeps its size and the background flattens (the "vertigo" shot).swapDirection
[[Dolly Track Along Curves]]Slides the camera along one curve and the target along another.Camera Curve, Target Curve, t1, t2
[[Animate To Plan]]Swings up over the target into a top view.distance
[[Animate To Elevation]]Swings down to the target's height for an elevation view.distance
src = GetCameraFromViewport
t   = slider 0.3 live
orb = OrbitCamera
cam = ApplyCamera [camera]

src.camera -> orb.camera
t ~> orb.t
orb.camera -> cam.camera

Chaining moves

Because every move starts from the camera it is given, you can chain them: feed the output of one into the next, and give each its own event from an [[Event Timeline]]. Here the camera flies through the keyframes over the first 70% of the animation, then swings up into plan.

t   = slider 0.5 live
ev  = EventTimeline [t] events="fly: 0-0.7, plan: 0.7-1"
nv  = GetCameraFromNamedviews [namedViews]
key = AnimateKeyframes [cameras, t]
pl  = AnimateToPlan
cam = ApplyCamera [camera]

t ~> ev.t
nv.camera -> key.cameras
ev.event1 ~> key.t
key.camera -> pl.camera
ev.event2 ~> pl.t
pl.camera -> cam.camera

Parallel views

[[Animate To Plan]] and [[Animate To Elevation]] work in perspective and parallel viewports; [[Dolly Zoom]] needs a perspective view because it changes the lens. For parallel views, [[Set Frustum]] zooms the active viewport in or out around a target point.
